## What the live runs proved, and what they revealed

1. **The live path works end to end.** Real search, real fetching under the SSRF and robots rules, real extraction and de-duplication, and a real strict-schema model call.
2. **The deterministic validator does its job against a real model.** Both runs were rejected by our own checks, not by the provider, and both failed closed to `needs_review` with the analysis withheld. This is the behaviour the fixtures assert, now observed against live output.
3. **Two fixes came out of the live runs**, both in this task's commit:
   - Groq's constrained decoder rejects a JSON schema containing `pattern` (`json_validate_failed`), so `pattern` is stripped from the schema sent over the wire while the same strict Pydantic models still enforce it on the response. Nothing is relaxed.
   - The discovery analysis token cap of 2,500 truncated a five-source run, which the provider also reports as a failed generation. Measured usage is about 1,750 completion tokens for two sources, so the cap is now 6,000.
4. **Expect `needs_review` on real Nigerian news pages.** Those pages routinely name serving officials, and the safety rules forbid publishing an analysis that identifies people. The reviewer workflow is the intended path for such a run; no analysis reaches the public record without a human decision. If that proves too strict for named public officials in official announcements, it is a product decision for the maintainer, not a validator to loosen quietly.

## Known limitations

- Groq's free tier rate-limits quickly. A live evaluation over the 36-case golden corpus returned `provider_unavailable` for 25 cases (see `docs/evidence/BE-085-live-evaluation.md`).
- Local live runs used the local development database with seeded public projects and no private reports.
- Provider-side retention and terms for Groq and Brave have not been reviewed; that remains a production gate item.
